# Pressure ladder: current model

The ladder is a local exploratory case for the active diagram/test and SOP sequence. Nominal hotfire opens feed valves, closes valves marked as ambient vents, and uses diagram regulator pressures (or SOP initial regulator settings). Missing nominal setpoints must be entered in the case. Initial state and each cumulative SOP step remain selectable. Failure overrides force valve positions locally and do not change the diagram, SOP, or Boolean failure tree.

Choose a source, destination, and connected process route. Actuator edges and regulator control ports are excluded. Check valves follow local left-to-right direction; existing regulators use source-derived process direction between their left/right ports. Numbered continuations form a zero-length connection only when exactly two markers match. Unlike-fluid tank boundaries are not implicitly coupled. Route search is bounded; this is not a simultaneous branched-network solution. The prescribed flow applies to the chosen route only.

Physical tubing inputs live in `edge.hydraulics`: `lengthM`, `idIn`, `material` (`stainless` or `copper`), and `roughnessMm`. They are editable in either diagram detail mode and persist through normal edge autosave, collaboration, undo, test copies, and JSON round trips. Physical length is divided proportionally when a rendered pipe is split; drawing scale is never used to assign an initially unknown physical length. The ladder uses diagram fields by default. Missing physical lengths evenly share the user-entered total length of unspecified segments; known lengths are additional. Entire-route override ignores diagram line fields. Other case settings last for the mounted ladder session only.

No positive Cv means no additional component loss or component length. Tubing friction remains. Entered positive Cv uses the equivalent water-calibrated area `Cv × 1.697e-5 m²`, with `ΔP = mdot²/(2 rho area²)` as a low pressure-drop approximation. Regulators impose ideal nominal pressure without droop or capacity modeling. A setting above available inlet pressure is unavailable, rather than clamped into an apparently feasible operating point. Closed components leave downstream pressure unknown because gas may remain trapped.

Nitrogen density, viscosity and sound speed are interpolated from a bundled CoolProp 8.0.0 HEOS table at 5 K / 2 bar spacing (plus atmospheric pressure), bounded to 200–500 K and atmospheric–400 bar absolute. Regenerate with `python scripts/generate-nitrogen-properties.py` with CoolProp installed. The browser needs no Python service. Darcy friction uses laminar 64/Re, Colebrook turbulent and transition interpolation. Segment properties use inlet conditions. Mach ≥ 0.3 or cumulative friction pressure drop ≥ 10% of the source/regulator absolute inlet pressure makes downstream results unavailable. These bounds do not constitute validation of a design. Other fluids require explicit confirmation of single-phase liquid behavior and entered constant density and viscosity; no other gas or two-phase model is supplied.

Temperature sweep evaluates the same flow, route and tubing case over up to 101 temperatures and exports detailed CSV rows, inputs and diagnostics. Liquid properties remain the entered constants across the sweep.

Vent time is a separate hypothetical isolated-reservoir estimate. The user supplies trapped nitrogen volume, initial/target gauge pressures, effective vent diameter and discharge coefficient. It integrates the choked/subcritical compressible-orifice mass flow for an isothermal ideal-nitrogen reservoir (`R=296.803 J/kg/K`, `gamma=1.4`). It excludes replenishment, cooling and downstream tubing losses. The effective vent opening must be supplied; it is not inferred from a nominal tube diameter or gas Cv. Target must be above atmosphere; a steady-state isolated region never supplies a fabricated initial trapped pressure. The topology indication reflects open paths from the selected source to ambient; it is not a transient pressure simulation or a clearance indication.

References: [CoolProp nitrogen](https://coolprop.org/fluid_properties/fluids/Nitrogen.html), [NASA compressible mass flow](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/mass-flow-rate-equations/), and Daniel's supplied `nitrogen_pressure_drop_sweep.py` / `nitrogen_pressure_drop_tool.py` for friction methodology and example tube IDs. Material roughness presets are editable assumptions, not material certifications.

Verification: `node scripts/verify-pressure-model.mjs` compares independent Python/CoolProp reference calculations (maximum pressure-drop difference 0.012% in the checked 250–350 K cases), checks isolation, direction, regulator feasibility, geometry overrides and invalid inputs, and compares vent integration against an independent pressure-coordinate integral. `check-planning.mjs`, `check-collaboration.mjs`, and `check-pipe-split.mjs` cover physical-input persistence, import validation, collaboration and conserved split length. Run `npm run build` before committing.
